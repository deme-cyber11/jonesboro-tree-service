After a tree is removed, the stump left behind is more than just an eyesore. It can attract termites, harbor fungal diseases, send up unwanted sprouts, and create a tripping hazard in your yard. Professional stump grinding eliminates the stump entirely, grinding it 6-12 inches below the soil surface so the area can be filled, graded, and returned to usable lawn or garden space.

How Stump Grinding Works

Stump grinding is a straightforward but specialized process that requires commercial-grade equipment and experienced operators. Here is what to expect when you hire Jonesboro Tree Pros:

  1. Pre-grinding assessment -- we check the stump diameter, root flare size, and proximity to underground utilities (water lines, gas lines, irrigation, and buried cables). We call Arkansas 811 to mark utility locations before any grinding begins if needed.
  2. Equipment positioning -- we bring a commercial stump grinder appropriate for the stump size. For stumps in tight spaces like fenced backyards, we use compact track-mounted grinders that fit through standard gates. For large stumps in open areas, we use full-size self-propelled grinders for faster processing.
  3. Grinding below grade -- the grinder uses a high-speed carbide-tipped cutting wheel to chip away the stump and major surface roots. We grind to a depth of 6-12 inches below grade, which is deep enough to allow grass to grow over the area or to plant new landscaping.
  4. Chip management -- grinding produces wood chips and soil. We can either backfill the hole with the chips (which decompose over time and settle naturally) or haul the chips away and backfill with topsoil for a cleaner finish.
  5. Site cleanup -- we rake the area smooth, clean up any scattered chips, and leave the site ready for whatever you plan to do with the space.

Why You Should Remove That Old Stump

Many homeowners in Jonesboro live with old tree stumps in their yards for years, but there are several practical reasons to have them ground out:

  • Termite and pest attraction -- decaying wood is a magnet for termites, carpenter ants, and wood-boring beetles. A rotting stump near your home can serve as a bridge for termites to reach your foundation.
  • Fungal disease spread -- stumps can harbor root rot fungi like Armillaria that spread underground through root grafts to infect healthy nearby trees.
  • Unwanted sprouting -- many hardwood species, especially sweetgums and elms common in Jonesboro, send up aggressive root suckers from the stump. These sprouts can pop up across your yard and become a constant maintenance headache.
  • Tripping hazard -- stumps at or near ground level are a liability, especially for children, elderly family members, and visitors who may not see them in the grass.
  • Mowing obstruction -- stumps force you to mow around them, leaving uneven patches and making lawn care more time-consuming.
  • Property value -- a yard full of old stumps looks neglected. Removing them is one of the simplest ways to improve your home's curb appeal.

Stump Grinding vs. Stump Removal: What Is the Difference?

These terms are often used interchangeably, but they describe different processes:

  • Stump grinding uses a machine to chip the stump and surface roots into small pieces below ground level. The root system remains in the ground and decomposes naturally over several years. This is the preferred method for most residential properties because it is faster, less invasive, and more affordable.
  • Full stump removal (extraction) involves digging out the entire stump and root ball using heavy machinery. This method is more disruptive to the surrounding landscape and is typically only necessary for construction projects where excavation requires a completely clear root zone.

For most Jonesboro homeowners, stump grinding is the right choice. It is efficient, affordable, and leaves your yard ready for use within the same day. If you need full extraction for a construction project, we can handle that through our land clearing service.
