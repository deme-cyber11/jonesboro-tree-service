Types of Tree Trimming We Offer

Different trees and situations call for different trimming approaches. Our arborists assess each tree individually and recommend the right method based on the species, age, condition, and your goals.

  • Crown cleaning -- removal of dead, dying, diseased, and broken branches from the crown. This is the most common type of trimming and is essential for maintaining tree health and preventing hazardous deadfall.
  • Crown thinning -- selective removal of interior branches to reduce density, improve light penetration, and allow air movement through the canopy. This reduces wind resistance and lowers the risk of storm damage, which is especially important during the severe weather season in Craighead County.
  • Crown raising (canopy lifting) -- removal of lower branches to increase clearance over sidewalks, driveways, rooflines, and structures. A common request for homeowners in Jonesboro neighborhoods where mature oaks and sweetgums hang low over yards.
  • Crown reduction -- reducing the overall size of a tree by cutting back to lateral branches. This is the proper alternative to topping (which we never do) and is used when a tree has outgrown its space or is interfering with power lines.
  • Vista pruning -- selective thinning to open up views without significantly altering the tree's shape. Popular for properties along Crowley's Ridge and other scenic areas.
  • Structural pruning -- corrective trimming for young trees to establish strong branch architecture and prevent future problems. This is one of the best investments you can make for newly planted trees.

When to Trim Your Trees

The best time to trim most trees in the Jonesboro area depends on the species and the reason for pruning:

  • Late winter (January-March) -- ideal for most hardwoods, including oaks, maples, and sweetgums. Trees are dormant, making it easier to see the branch structure, and wounds seal faster when spring growth begins.
  • After spring bloom -- flowering trees like dogwoods, redbuds, and crabapples should be pruned right after they finish blooming so you do not remove next year's flower buds.
  • Summer -- light trimming and deadwood removal can be done anytime. This is also the best time to identify and remove branches with disease symptoms that may not be visible during dormancy.
  • Any time for hazards -- dead, broken, or hanging branches should be removed as soon as they are noticed, regardless of the season. Safety comes first.

We strongly advise against topping trees. Topping causes rapid, weak regrowth that is more susceptible to breakage, disease, and decay. It also permanently disfigures the tree. Our ISA-certified arborists use only industry-approved pruning methods.

Benefits of Professional Tree Trimming

Investing in regular tree trimming pays dividends for your property in several ways:

  • Prevents property damage -- dead and overextended limbs are the leading cause of tree-related property damage during storms. Proper trimming reduces this risk dramatically.
  • Improves tree health -- removing dead and diseased wood stops decay from spreading and redirects the tree's energy toward healthy growth.
  • Increases curb appeal -- well-maintained trees are one of the most impactful elements of a home's landscape. They can add 10-15% to property values.
  • Provides clearance -- keeps branches away from your roof, gutters, siding, power lines, and walkways.
  • Improves sunlight -- thinning allows more light to reach your lawn, garden beds, and lower plantings.

Frequently Asked Questions

Most mature trees benefit from pruning every 3 to 5 years. Fast-growing species like silver maple or sweetgum may need attention every 2 to 3 years. Young trees should be pruned annually to establish strong branch structure.

Trimming generally refers to cutting back overgrown branches for clearance and aesthetics. Pruning is a more targeted practice focused on tree health, removing dead, diseased, or crossing branches. Our arborists perform both using ANSI A300 standards.

Late winter through early spring, while trees are dormant, is ideal for most species. However, dead or hazardous branches should be removed any time of year. Our arborists can recommend the best timing for your specific trees.
