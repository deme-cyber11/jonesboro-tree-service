Not every tree that looks unhealthy needs to be removed, and not every tree that looks healthy is safe. A professional tree health assessment from an ISA-certified arborist gives you a clear, accurate picture of what is going on with your trees -- and what, if anything, needs to be done about it. At Delta Tree Doctors, we pride ourselves on giving honest assessments, recommending treatment over removal whenever the tree can be saved.

What We Evaluate During a Tree Health Assessment

Our arborists conduct a thorough, systematic evaluation of your tree from the root zone to the crown. Here is what we examine:

  • Crown condition -- we assess leaf density, color, size, and distribution. Sparse canopies, undersized leaves, early leaf drop, and discoloration are all indicators of underlying stress or disease.
  • Trunk integrity -- we inspect for cracks, cavities, bulges, cankers, and signs of internal decay. For trees with suspected internal damage, we may use a sounding mallet or resistance drill to assess wood density without harming the tree.
  • Root zone health -- we check for heaving soil, exposed roots, fungal fruiting bodies (mushrooms) at the base, girdling roots, and signs of root rot. Root problems are one of the leading causes of unexpected tree failure.
  • Branch architecture -- we evaluate branch attachment strength, looking for included bark, co-dominant stems, overextended limbs, and dead branches that could fail in a storm.
  • Pest identification -- we look for evidence of boring insects, leaf-feeding caterpillars, scale insects, and other pests that affect trees in the Jonesboro area. Early identification is critical for effective treatment.
  • Disease diagnosis -- we identify fungal, bacterial, and viral diseases based on visible symptoms, fruiting bodies, and canopy patterns. Common diagnoses in our area include bacterial leaf scorch, oak wilt, anthracnose, and various root rots.
  • Site conditions -- we assess soil compaction, drainage, grade changes, construction damage, and other environmental factors that may be stressing the tree.

Common Tree Health Issues in Jonesboro and Northeast Arkansas

The climate and geography of Craighead County create specific conditions that affect tree health. Our arborists regularly diagnose and treat the following issues in the Jonesboro area:

  • Bacterial leaf scorch -- a chronic, progressive disease that affects oaks, sycamores, and elms. Symptoms include brown, scorched leaf margins that appear in mid to late summer and worsen each year. There is no cure, but proper care can extend the tree's functional life by years.
  • Hypoxylon canker -- a fungal disease that attacks stressed hardwoods, especially post oaks and red oaks. The bark peels away to reveal dark, crusty fungal mats. Trees with advanced Hypoxylon are structurally compromised and typically require removal.
  • Iron chlorosis -- common in Jonesboro's alkaline clay soils. Affected trees show yellowing leaves with green veins (interveinal chlorosis), especially pin oaks and river birch. Soil amendments and trunk injections can correct the deficiency.
  • Storm damage stress -- trees that survived severe weather events may have hidden internal damage that slowly worsens over time. Cracks that appeared minor during cleanup can expand and become failure points months or years later.
  • Emerald ash borer -- this invasive beetle has been confirmed in Arkansas and poses a serious threat to all ash trees. Early detection through assessment allows for protective treatments before infestation takes hold.
  • Root rot (Armillaria) -- a soil-borne fungal disease that is especially problematic in areas with poor drainage. It spreads through root contact and can affect multiple trees on a property.

Why ISA Certification Matters

The International Society of Arboriculture (ISA) certification is the gold standard in the tree care industry. An ISA-certified arborist has passed a rigorous exam covering tree biology, diagnosis, pruning science, soil management, safety, and ethics. They are required to maintain their certification through ongoing education.

When you hire an ISA-certified arborist for a health assessment, you are getting a diagnosis based on science, not guesswork. Our arborists will not recommend removal if treatment is viable, and they will not push unnecessary services. You get an honest evaluation and a clear path forward for each tree on your property.
