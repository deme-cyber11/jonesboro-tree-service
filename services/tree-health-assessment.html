ISA-Certified Arborist Tree Health Assessments in Jonesboro, Arkansas

Your trees are among the most valuable assets on your property. A single mature oak can be worth $10,000 or more in real estate value, and it takes decades to replace. That is why proactive health assessments are so important. At Jonesboro Tree Pros, our ISA-Certified Arborists perform comprehensive tree health evaluations that identify problems early, when they are most treatable and least expensive to address. Every assessment concludes with a written report detailing our findings, diagnosis, and specific recommendations for treatment or management.

Northeast Arkansas's climate creates favorable conditions for a wide range of tree diseases and pest populations. Hot, humid summers promote fungal infections, while mild winters allow pest populations to survive and thrive year after year. Our arborists have deep knowledge of the diseases, insects, and environmental stressors that specifically affect trees in Craighead County and the surrounding region.

What Our Assessment Covers

A tree health assessment from Jonesboro Tree Pros is a thorough, science-based evaluation of your tree's condition from root to crown. Our arborists examine:

  • Canopy Health — We evaluate leaf color, density, and size. Thinning canopies, premature leaf drop, discoloration, and dieback are indicators of disease, nutrient deficiency, or root problems.
  • Trunk and Bark Condition — Cracks, cankers, peeling bark, cavities, and fungal fruiting bodies (mushrooms or conks) on the trunk indicate structural decay or active disease.
  • Branch Structure — We assess the tree's overall architecture, looking for co-dominant stems, included bark, deadwood, crossing branches, and other structural weaknesses that increase failure risk.
  • Root Zone and Root Flare — Soil compaction, girdling roots, root rot, and changes in grade around the base of the tree can all compromise stability and health. We examine the root flare and surrounding soil for signs of trouble.
  • Pest Activity — We look for evidence of boring insects (exit holes, sawdust frass), defoliating caterpillars, scale insects, spider mites, and other pests that attack trees common in Jonesboro.
  • Disease Symptoms — Our arborists can identify oak wilt, anthracnose, bacterial leaf scorch, pine wilt disease, fire blight, and many other conditions that affect Arkansas trees.
  • Environmental Stress — Construction damage, soil compaction, drainage changes, herbicide exposure, and other environmental factors can slowly kill trees without obvious symptoms. We evaluate the tree's site conditions as part of every assessment.

Signs Your Tree Needs an Assessment

Trees often show warning signs before a problem becomes severe. Schedule an assessment if you notice any of the following on your property:

  • Leaves turning yellow, brown, or wilting outside of normal fall color change
  • Premature leaf drop in spring or summer
  • Dead branches appearing in the canopy
  • Mushrooms, conks, or other fungal growth on the trunk or at the base
  • Bark peeling, cracking, or falling off in large pieces
  • Small holes in the bark with sawdust-like material around them
  • A sudden lean or visible root lifting on one side
  • Significantly less foliage than in previous years
  • Oozing sap or dark streaks running down the trunk
  • Construction activity, grading, or digging near the tree within the past one to three years

Common Tree Diseases in Jonesboro, AR

Our arborists regularly diagnose and treat the following conditions in Craighead County:

  • Oak Wilt — A lethal fungal disease that clogs the water-conducting vessels in oaks. Red oaks can die within weeks of infection. Early detection and treatment with fungicide injections can save white oaks if caught in time.
  • Anthracnose — A common fungal disease that causes irregular brown spots and leaf curling on sycamores, dogwoods, and maples. Usually treatable with pruning and fungicide.
  • Pine Bark Beetle — Boring insects that attack stressed pines, tunneling under the bark and introducing blue stain fungus. Infested pines often die within weeks. Preventive insecticide treatments can protect high-value pines.
  • Bacterial Leaf Scorch — A chronic bacterial infection spread by leafhoppers that causes progressive browning of leaf margins on oaks, elms, and sycamores. There is no cure, but antibiotic injections can extend the tree's productive life by several years.
  • Hypoxylon Canker — A fungal disease that appears as dark, crusty growths on the bark of stress-weakened hardwoods. By the time fruiting bodies are visible, the tree is usually in severe decline.

Preventive Care Saves Money

A tree health assessment is one of the most cost-effective investments in your property's landscape. Catching a disease in its early stages often means the difference between a $200 treatment and a $2,000 removal. Identifying a structurally compromised tree before a storm saves you from emergency removal costs, property damage, and potential liability. We recommend scheduling a comprehensive assessment every two to three years for mature trees, or annually for trees that have had previous health concerns.

Tree Health Assessment FAQs

Common questions about tree health assessment services in Jonesboro, AR.

What does a tree health assessment include?

Our tree health assessment includes a thorough visual inspection of the tree's canopy, trunk, root flare, and surrounding soil by an ISA-Certified Arborist. We check for signs of disease, pest infestation, structural defects, root damage, and environmental stress. You receive a written report with our findings, diagnosis, and specific treatment or management recommendations.

How do I know if my tree needs a health assessment?

Common signs that your tree needs a professional assessment include yellowing or wilting leaves outside of fall, premature leaf drop, dead branches in the canopy, peeling or cracking bark, mushrooms or fungal growth at the base, visible pest activity, leaning, and soil heaving around the roots. If you notice any of these signs, schedule an assessment before the problem worsens.

Can a sick tree be saved?

Many tree diseases and pest problems can be treated successfully when caught early. Treatments may include targeted pruning, fungicide or insecticide application, soil amendments, or improved drainage. However, some conditions like advanced oak wilt or severe structural decay may be beyond treatment, in which case we will recommend removal for safety. Early detection through regular assessments is the best way to save your trees.

How often should I have my trees assessed?

We recommend a professional tree health assessment every two to three years for mature trees, or annually for trees that have had previous health issues. You should also schedule an assessment after major storm events, if you notice sudden changes in your tree's appearance, or before purchasing a property with large, mature trees.
