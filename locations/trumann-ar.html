Complete Tree Care for Trumann and Poinsett County

Trumann is a tight-knit community of approximately 7,000 residents located in Poinsett County, about 25 miles south of Jonesboro along Highway 63. The town serves as a local hub for the surrounding agricultural area and is known for its friendly neighborhoods, community spirit, and the kind of spacious properties where large trees are a defining feature of the landscape. From the established residential streets near downtown to the sprawling rural lots that stretch out toward the county line, Trumann is a place where trees are both valued and, at times, a source of concern.

Many properties in and around Trumann sit on generous lots — half-acre, one-acre, or larger — with mature trees that have been growing for decades. Pecan trees planted generations ago tower over backyards. Large sweetgums and oaks shade front yards and line fence rows. Pin oaks and hackberries grow thick along drainage ditches and property boundaries. These trees provide shade, privacy, and beauty, but they also demand regular maintenance. Without periodic trimming, branches grow over rooflines, interfere with power lines, and develop weak attachment points that become dangerous during storms.

The rural character of Poinsett County means that many property owners face tree care challenges that go beyond a single yard tree. Overgrown fence rows choked with volunteer hackberries and elms need to be cleared to maintain property boundaries. Wooded areas along field edges may have dead or leaning trees that threaten outbuildings, equipment, or pasture fencing. And when severe weather moves through — as it regularly does across the flat Delta terrain of eastern Arkansas — large trees on rural properties can come down across driveways, farm roads, and utility lines, creating urgent situations that require professional equipment and expertise to resolve.

Jonesboro Tree Pros is just a 25-minute drive from Trumann down Highway 63, making us one of the closest professional tree service providers available. Our crews are familiar with the area and understand the specific needs of Poinsett County property owners, whether you need a single stump ground in your backyard or an entire tree line cleared along the edge of your farm.

The ideal time for most tree trimming in the Trumann area is during the dormant season, typically late fall through early spring (November through March). During dormancy, trees have dropped their leaves, making it easier to see the branch structure and identify problems. Pruning during this period also reduces stress on the tree and minimizes the risk of pest and disease exposure at the cut sites. That said, deadwood removal and emergency trimming can be done safely at any time of year.
