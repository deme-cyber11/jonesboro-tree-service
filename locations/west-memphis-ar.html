Storm-Ready Tree Care for West Memphis and Crittenden County

West Memphis is the largest city in Crittenden County, with a population of approximately 24,000 residents. Situated directly across the Mississippi River from Memphis, Tennessee, West Memphis occupies a unique position at the crossroads of Interstate 40 and Interstate 55 — making it one of the most important transportation hubs in eastern Arkansas. The city's proximity to the river, its flat Delta terrain, and its exposure to severe weather from the Mississippi River valley make professional tree care not just a convenience, but a necessity for local property owners.

The Mississippi River floodplain that surrounds West Memphis supports a distinct mix of tree species adapted to the low-lying, moisture-rich soils. Cottonwood, bald cypress, water oak, willow oak, silver maple, and pecan trees are all common throughout the area. Many of these species grow quickly and develop large canopies, but their wood can be brittle, and their shallow root systems make them vulnerable to high winds. When a severe thunderstorm or tornado moves through Crittenden County — and these storms hit the Delta with regularity — the result is often widespread tree damage that demands immediate professional attention.

West Memphis homeowners along Broadway Avenue, the neighborhoods near Southland Casino Racing, and the residential areas off East Service Road all face tree-related challenges unique to this region. Standing water after heavy rains can saturate root zones and cause otherwise healthy trees to lean or fall. Older neighborhoods often have trees that have grown unchecked for decades, with limbs extending over rooflines, interfering with power lines, or encroaching on neighboring properties. Our crew at Jonesboro Tree Pros has the equipment and expertise to handle every one of these situations safely and efficiently.

Emergency tree service is our most-requested offering in the West Memphis area. The combination of Delta storm exposure and fast-growing, weak-wooded tree species means that fallen trees blocking driveways, crushing fences, and damaging roofs are a regular occurrence after major weather events. Our emergency response team is available around the clock to help West Memphis residents secure their properties and clear dangerous debris when storms strike.
