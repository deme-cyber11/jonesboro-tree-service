Forrest City is the county seat of St. Francis County and an important stop along the Interstate 40 corridor in eastern Arkansas, situated approximately 55 miles south of Jonesboro. With a population of around 14,000, the city serves as the commercial center for the surrounding agricultural region and is the closest gateway to Village Creek State Park — one of the most popular outdoor recreation destinations in the Arkansas Delta. The combination of fertile alluvial soils, flat terrain, and abundant rainfall creates ideal growing conditions for both native hardwoods and fast-spreading volunteer species that demand regular professional management.

Village Creek State Park, located just a few miles south of town, showcases the type of dense bottomland hardwood forest that once covered much of eastern Arkansas. Towering sweetgums, water oaks, overcup oaks, and bald cypress fill the park, and many of those same species have established themselves throughout Forrest City's residential neighborhoods and along the city's main streets. In the older sections of town near Washington Street and the Forrest City Civic Center, pecan trees and post oaks planted decades ago have reached impressive sizes, providing welcome shade in the summer but also creating risk when storms roll through the flat, exposed delta landscape.

The East Arkansas delta is one of the most storm-vulnerable regions in the state. Without the topographic protection that hills and ridges provide, Forrest City is fully exposed to straight-line winds, derechos, and supercell thunderstorms that move across the agricultural flatlands with little resistance. The same conditions make ice storms particularly damaging, as heavy ice accumulation on large canopies can snap limbs and split trunks across entire neighborhoods. Our 24/7 emergency response crew deploys crane trucks, bucket trucks, and commercial chippers to restore safety after major weather events in Forrest City and throughout St. Francis County.
