Expert Tree Care for Lake City and the Cache River Area

Lake City is a small, close-knit community of approximately 2,500 residents located in Craighead County, about 13 miles east of Jonesboro along Highway 18. Situated near the Cache River — one of Arkansas's most ecologically significant waterways and a Ramsar-designated Wetland of International Importance — Lake City is surrounded by some of the most densely wooded bottomland in the region. The combination of thick tree cover, low-lying terrain, and exposure to severe Delta weather creates a steady need for professional tree care among local property owners.

The trees in and around Lake City are shaped by the Cache River floodplain. Bottomland hardwood species dominate the landscape: towering bald cypress trees grow along the river's edge, while water oaks, overcup oaks, sweetgum, green ash, and pecan trees fill the surrounding lowlands and residential yards. Many of these trees grow rapidly in the rich, moisture-laden soils, developing large canopies that provide welcome shade but also create challenges when they grow too close to homes, sheds, power lines, and property boundaries.

Storm damage is a recurring problem for Lake City residents. The flat, open terrain of eastern Craighead County offers little resistance to the severe thunderstorms and straight-line winds that sweep through the area during spring and summer. Trees with root systems compromised by saturated soils are especially vulnerable to being uprooted during high winds. When these storms pass through, they leave behind a trail of snapped trunks, downed limbs, and toppled trees that block driveways, damage roofs, and knock out power lines. Our emergency response team at Jonesboro Tree Pros is just a short drive away and ready to help Lake City residents recover quickly after severe weather events.

For homeowners and landowners who want to take a proactive approach, regular tree trimming goes a long way toward reducing storm damage risk. Removing deadwood, thinning heavy canopies, and improving branch structure all help trees better withstand high winds. Our ISA-certified arborists can also assess individual trees for signs of root compromise, internal decay, or disease that might not be visible from the ground — catching problems before they turn into emergencies.
