Tree Service in Brookland, Arkansas

Just 8 miles from Jonesboro — fast, professional tree care for Brookland's growing community.

Brookland is one of the fastest-growing communities in Craighead County, located just 8 miles north of Jonesboro along the Highway 49 corridor. What was once a quiet farming town has transformed into a thriving bedroom community that families are drawn to for its top-rated Brookland School District, safe neighborhoods, and small-town atmosphere — all within a short commute of the employment, shopping, and dining options in Jonesboro. That rapid residential growth has created a strong and ongoing demand for professional tree care as new subdivisions are carved from wooded tracts and established neighborhoods maintain aging yard trees.

The older sections of Brookland along Main Street and around the original town center feature mature shade trees that have been part of the community for generations. Large pecans, white oaks, red oaks, and sweetgums shade front yards and line the streets, providing character and property value that homeowners rightly want to protect. These heritage trees require periodic structural pruning to remove deadwood, reduce weight on overextended limbs, and address co-dominant stems that can split during wind events. Our ISA-certified arborists assess the health and structure of each tree before recommending any work, ensuring that we preserve as much of the canopy as possible while eliminating genuine hazards.

In Brookland's newer subdivisions, builders have often left clusters of existing trees in place during construction — a welcome practice that gives new neighborhoods an instant sense of maturity. However, construction activity can damage root zones through soil compaction, grade changes, and severed roots that do not show symptoms for two to three years after the damage occurs. We recommend post-construction tree health assessments for any Brookland property where grading, trenching, or heavy equipment operation occurred near existing trees. Early detection of construction-related stress can save trees that might otherwise decline and require expensive removal down the road.

Brookland's position along Highway 49, at the transition from the higher ground of Crowley's Ridge to the agricultural lowlands, means the local tree population includes both upland and lowland species. Hickories, post oaks, and eastern red cedars occupy the ridge soils on the west side of town, while water oaks, silver maples, and green ash favor the lower elevations to the east. This species diversity is one of the things that makes Brookland an attractive place to live, but it also means that a one-size-fits-all approach to tree care will not work. Every species has different pruning requirements, disease vulnerabilities, and structural tendencies that an ISA-certified arborist understands and accounts for.
