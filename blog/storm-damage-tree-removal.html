What to Do After Storm Damage to Trees in Arkansas

A step-by-step guide for homeowners dealing with storm-damaged trees in Jonesboro and northeast Arkansas.

  1. Home
  2. Blog
  3. Storm Damage to Trees in Arkansas
March 5, 2025 9 min read Emergency Service

Arkansas is no stranger to severe weather. From the powerful spring thunderstorms that roll through Craighead County to the occasional ice storms in winter and the ever-present tornado threat, our trees take a beating. When a storm strikes and leaves behind broken limbs, uprooted trees, or dangling branches over your home, it is natural to feel overwhelmed. The most important thing is knowing what to do first and what to avoid.

This guide walks you through the steps to take after storm damage to your trees, from immediate safety precautions to working with your insurance company and hiring a professional tree service.

Understanding Arkansas Storm Season

Northeast Arkansas, including Jonesboro and the surrounding Craighead County area, experiences several types of severe weather that can damage trees:

  • Spring thunderstorms (March through June): These bring straight-line winds that frequently exceed 60 mph, heavy rain, and hail. Straight-line winds are the most common cause of tree damage in our area, snapping trunks, shearing off large branches, and uprooting shallow-rooted trees.
  • Tornadoes: Arkansas averages around 30 tornadoes per year, and the northeast corner of the state sits squarely in the risk zone. Tornado damage to trees can be catastrophic, with entire trees twisted off at the base or thrown across properties.
  • Ice storms (December through February): Ice accumulation of even half an inch can add hundreds of pounds of weight to a tree's canopy, causing branches to snap and entire trees to split. The January 2009 ice storm remains one of the most damaging weather events in northeast Arkansas history.
  • Summer heat and drought stress: While not as dramatic as tornadoes, prolonged heat and drought weaken trees over time, making them more vulnerable to wind damage when storms do arrive.

Step 1: Prioritize Safety

After a storm passes, your first priority is keeping your family safe. Do not rush outside to assess tree damage, especially at night or during ongoing weather. Wait until conditions are safe before surveying your property.

Critical safety rules:

  • Stay away from downed power lines. If a tree has fallen on a power line or brought a line down, stay at least 35 feet away and call Entergy Arkansas at 1-800-368-3749 immediately. Downed lines can be energized even if they are not sparking. Never attempt to move a tree or limb that is in contact with a power line.
  • Do not go under hanging branches. Partially broken branches that are still attached to the tree (called "hangers") can fall without warning. If you see limbs dangling overhead, do not stand or park beneath them.
  • Watch for leaning trees. A tree that has been partially uprooted may look stable but can topple at any time, especially if the wind picks up again. Keep a distance equal to at least the height of the tree.
  • Check your roof from the ground first. If a tree or large limb has struck your home, assess the situation from outside before going inside. Structural damage may make the interior unsafe.

Step 2: Document the Damage

Before any cleanup begins, take photos and video of all damage. This documentation is essential for insurance claims. Walk around your property (maintaining safe distances from hazards) and photograph:

  • Every damaged tree from multiple angles
  • Any damage to your home, garage, fence, vehicles, or other structures
  • Debris fields showing the extent of the storm's impact
  • Damage to neighboring properties caused by your trees (or vice versa)
  • Any pre-existing conditions that might be relevant (a tree that was already leaning, for instance)

Take these photos in daylight when possible, and save them in a separate folder on your phone or computer. Timestamp everything. Your insurance adjuster will need this documentation, and having thorough records from the start will make the claims process much smoother.

Step 3: Assess the Damage

Not every storm-damaged tree needs to be removed. Trees are remarkably resilient, and many can recover from significant damage with proper care. Here is how to evaluate your situation:

Trees That Can Likely Be Saved

  • Trees that lost less than 25% of their canopy and still have their main structure intact
  • Trees with broken branches that can be cleanly pruned back to the trunk or a lateral branch
  • Young, vigorous trees that have been bent but not snapped
  • Trees with minor crown damage and an otherwise healthy root system

Trees That Likely Need Removal

  • Trees that have been uprooted or have exposed, broken root systems
  • Trees with split trunks (the main trunk has cracked into two or more sections)
  • Trees that have lost more than 50% of their crown
  • Trees that have fallen onto structures, vehicles, or fences
  • Trees that are leaning significantly more than before the storm
  • Trees that were already in decline before the storm and have now sustained additional damage

Step 4: Know When to Call a Professional

There are some cleanup tasks you can safely handle yourself, and others that absolutely require a professional tree service.

You Can Handle It If:

  • Small branches (under 4 inches in diameter) are scattered on the ground
  • Leaf and twig debris needs to be raked up
  • A small tree or shrub has toppled and you can safely lift or drag it away

Call a Professional If:

  • Any tree or limb is in contact with or near power lines
  • Large branches are hanging in the canopy and could fall
  • A tree has fallen on your house, garage, or other structure
  • You need to use a chainsaw on anything larger than small limbs on the ground
  • A tree is partially uprooted and leaning
  • The work requires climbing or a ladder (never use a ladder for tree work)

After a major storm, demand for tree services spikes dramatically. Call a reputable company as soon as possible to get on the schedule. Be wary of out-of-town "storm chasers" who go door-to-door after severe weather. These operators often lack insurance, local knowledge, and accountability. Always verify that any tree service you hire is licensed, insured, and has a verifiable local presence.

Step 5: Filing Insurance Claims for Tree Damage

Understanding how homeowner's insurance handles tree damage can save you significant money and frustration. Here is what most Arkansas homeowners need to know:

What Is Typically Covered

  • Damage to structures: If a tree falls on your house, garage, shed, or fence, the structural damage is typically covered under your homeowner's policy.
  • Tree removal from structures: The cost to remove a tree that has fallen on an insured structure is usually covered, often up to $500 to $1,000 per tree depending on your policy.
  • Damage to vehicles: A tree falling on your car is typically covered under the comprehensive portion of your auto insurance, not your homeowner's policy.

What Is Typically NOT Covered

  • Tree removal from your yard: If a tree falls in your yard but does not hit any structure, most policies will not cover the removal cost. This is considered routine property maintenance.
  • Replacing the tree: Insurance typically does not pay to plant a replacement tree.
  • Pre-existing conditions: If your insurer determines the tree was dead or obviously hazardous before the storm, they may deny the claim.

Tips for a Smooth Claims Process

  1. Contact your insurer promptly. Most policies require you to report damage within a reasonable timeframe. Call your agent or the claims hotline as soon as you have documented the damage.
  2. Do not sign anything with a tree service until your adjuster has seen the damage, unless the situation is an immediate safety emergency. If you must authorize emergency work, keep detailed records and receipts.
  3. Get multiple quotes for tree removal and any structural repairs. Your insurer may require this.
  4. Save all receipts related to the storm damage, including temporary repairs, hotel stays (if your home is uninhabitable), and any emergency tree work.
  5. Know your deductible. If the total damage is close to your deductible, it may not be worth filing a claim.

Preparing Your Trees Before Storm Season

The best time to deal with storm damage is before it happens. Here are proactive steps Jonesboro homeowners can take:

  • Schedule annual tree inspections. An ISA-certified arborist can identify weak branches, structural defects, and disease problems before a storm turns them into emergencies.
  • Prune dead and weakened branches. Removing deadwood and crossing branches reduces the risk of breakage during high winds.
  • Thin dense canopies. Crown thinning allows wind to pass through the canopy rather than catching it like a sail.
  • Remove hazardous trees proactively. A tree that is already in decline is far more likely to fail during a storm. Removing it on your schedule is safer and less expensive than emergency removal.
  • Keep trees healthy. Proper watering during drought, mulching, and avoiding root damage all contribute to a tree's ability to withstand severe weather.
