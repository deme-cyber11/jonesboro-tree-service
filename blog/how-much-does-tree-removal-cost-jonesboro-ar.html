How Much Does Tree Removal Cost in Jonesboro, AR?

A complete 2026 pricing guide for homeowners in Jonesboro and Craighead County.

  1. Home
  2. /
  3. Blog
  4. /
  5. Tree Removal Cost Guide

Tree removal is one of the more significant outdoor maintenance expenses a homeowner faces, but understanding what drives the cost helps you budget properly and avoid overpaying. In Jonesboro, Arkansas, tree removal prices typically range from $400 to $2,500, depending on the size, species, location, and condition of the tree. This guide breaks down what you can expect to pay in 2026, what factors affect the final bill, and how to make sure you get the best value for your money.

Average Tree Removal Costs by Size

The single biggest factor in tree removal pricing is the size of the tree. Larger trees require more labor, specialized rigging, and heavier equipment. Here is what Jonesboro homeowners typically pay based on tree height:

Small Trees (Under 30 Feet) -- $400 to $700

Small trees include ornamental varieties like dogwoods, redbuds, and crape myrtles, as well as young pines and maples. These are generally straightforward removals that can be completed in a few hours by a small crew. Most small tree removals in the Jonesboro area fall between $400 and $700, including basic cleanup and debris hauling. If the tree is in an open yard with easy access, costs tend to land on the lower end of that range.

Medium Trees (30 to 60 Feet) -- $700 to $1,200

Medium-sized trees are the most common removal jobs in Craighead County. This category covers mature sweetgums, medium oaks, elms, and established pine trees. Removing a tree in this range typically requires a crew of three to four workers, a bucket truck or climbing gear, and half a day of work. Costs generally run between $700 and $1,200, though trees that are close to structures or power lines can push toward the higher end.

Large Trees (Over 60 Feet) -- $1,200 to $2,500

Large trees like mature oaks, tall pines, hickories, and century-old hardwoods are the most expensive to remove. These jobs involve extensive rigging, crane work in some cases, and a full day or more of labor. Costs range from $1,200 to $2,500, with the upper end reserved for very large specimens in tight spaces near homes, garages, or fences. A 90-foot oak leaning over a roof, for example, will cost significantly more than the same tree standing in the middle of an open field.

Factors That Affect Tree Removal Cost

Beyond tree size, several other variables influence the final price. Understanding these helps you anticipate your estimate and ask informed questions when comparing quotes.

Tree Location and Accessibility

A tree in the middle of a wide-open yard is far easier and cheaper to remove than one wedged between a house and a fence, overhanging a power line, or surrounded by landscaping that needs protection. Limited access increases labor time and may require specialized equipment. If a crane is needed to lift sections over a structure, that alone can add $500 to $1,000 to the total cost.

Tree Condition

Dead, dying, or structurally compromised trees can actually be more dangerous and difficult to remove than healthy ones. A dead tree with brittle wood is unpredictable when cut, requiring more careful rigging to prevent uncontrolled falls. Conversely, a healthy tree with a solid trunk can be felled in a more controlled manner. Trees with extensive rot, split trunks, or hanging dead limbs (widow-makers) may cost more due to the additional safety measures required.

Stump Removal

Most tree removal quotes cover cutting the tree down to a low stump and hauling away all debris. Stump grinding is typically a separate service, adding $150 to $500 depending on stump diameter and root spread. Some companies offer a discount if you bundle stump grinding with the tree removal. We recommend getting both done at the same time to avoid having to schedule and pay for a second visit.

Number of Trees

Removing multiple trees in a single visit is almost always cheaper per tree than removing them one at a time. The crew is already on site with all the necessary equipment, so the marginal cost of each additional tree drops. If you have several trees that need to come down, get a quote for the full batch rather than one at a time.

Emergency vs. Scheduled Removal

Emergency tree removal after a storm or sudden failure costs more than a scheduled job. Emergency calls involve after-hours response, rapid mobilization, and working in potentially hazardous post-storm conditions. Emergency tree service in Jonesboro typically ranges from $500 to $3,000, compared to the standard $400 to $2,500 for a planned removal. Whenever possible, schedule removals proactively rather than waiting for a crisis.

When Is Tree Removal Cheapest?

Timing your tree removal can save you money. Here are the best times to schedule the work:

  • Winter (December through February): Tree service companies in Jonesboro tend to be less busy during winter months when demand for trimming and lawn-adjacent services slows down. Many offer lower rates or are more willing to negotiate during this period. Leafless trees are also easier and faster to work on since there is less canopy weight and better visibility of the branch structure.
  • Non-emergency scheduling: Booking a removal two to three weeks in advance gives the company flexibility to schedule you during a slower window, which often results in better pricing compared to rush jobs.
  • Bundle with other work: If you also need trimming, stump grinding, or land clearing, bundling services into a single visit typically earns you a package discount.

What Is Included in the Price?

A reputable tree removal company in Jonesboro should include the following in their standard price:

  • Cutting and dismantling the tree: This includes felling the tree or sectional removal from the top down, depending on the situation.
  • Debris cleanup: All branches, limbs, and trunk sections should be removed from your property. Your yard should look cleaner when the crew leaves than when they arrived.
  • Haul-away: Debris is loaded onto trucks and hauled to a disposal or recycling facility. Some companies offer to leave firewood-length logs if you want them.
  • Low stump cut: The tree is cut as close to the ground as possible. Full stump grinding below grade is usually an add-on service.

Be wary of quotes that do not explicitly include cleanup and haul-away. A lowball price that leaves you with a yard full of debris is no bargain.

How to Get Accurate Estimates

The only way to get a reliable tree removal estimate is through an in-person assessment. No honest company can give you a firm price over the phone or from a photo. Here is how to make sure you get accurate, comparable quotes:

  • Get at least two to three estimates from licensed, insured companies. Compare scope of work, not just bottom-line price.
  • Ask what is included: Confirm that the quote covers cutting, cleanup, haul-away, and stump cut height. Ask about stump grinding as a separate line item.
  • Verify insurance: Any tree service working on your property should carry general liability insurance and workers' compensation. Ask for a certificate of insurance before work begins.
  • Check for licensing: Make sure the company is licensed to operate in Arkansas and has ISA-certified arborists on staff.
  • Read reviews: Look at Google reviews and ask for local references. A company's track record says more than their price.
